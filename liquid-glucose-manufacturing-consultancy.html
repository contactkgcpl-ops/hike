Frequently Asked Questions

Common queries about our liquid glucose manufacturing plant consultancy.

Liquid glucose is primarily manufactured from corn starch (maize starch), though tapioca starch, wheat starch, and potato starch can also be used. The starch undergoes controlled hydrolysis to produce glucose syrup of varying dextrose equivalents (DE). We guide you on raw material selection based on cost, availability, and end-product specifications.

Dextrose Equivalent (DE) measures the degree of starch hydrolysis — higher DE means more sweetness and lower viscosity. Confectionery uses typically require DE 38-42, while pharmaceutical applications may require different specifications. We guide you on producing the right DE grade for your target customers.

Key buyer industries include confectionery (candies, chocolates, chewing gum), bakery (breads, cakes), pharmaceuticals (cough syrups, tablet coating), beverages, ice cream, leather tanning, paper manufacturing, and tobacco. We help develop your multi-industry customer base.

Enzyme hydrolysis (using alpha-amylase and glucoamylase) produces higher quality, color-stable glucose with better yield, while acid hydrolysis is simpler and lower cost but produces slightly darker, less refined syrup. Modern plants predominantly use enzyme processes. We help you choose the right approach for your quality and budget targets.

Yes, we provide comprehensive guidance for producing pharmaceutical-grade liquid glucose meeting Indian Pharmacopoeia (IP) and British Pharmacopoeia (BP) standards, including GMP compliance, purity specifications, and quality testing protocols required for drug formulation applications.
